## background-and-culture

### [Pharisees](../articles/background-and-culture/pharisees.md) — Group

Read: Matthew 23:1-28; Mark 7:1-13; Acts 23:6-9; Philippians 3:5-6; Acts 5:34-39; John 3:1-2; Luke 13:31; Luke 7:36

Study questions:
- According to Acts 23:6-8, which beliefs did the Pharisees hold that the Sadducees rejected?
- In Matthew 23:2-3, what does Jesus tell the crowds to do with the Pharisees' teaching, and what does he warn them not to copy?
- Nicodemus (John 3:1-2) and Gamaliel (Acts 5:34-39) were both Pharisees. How do their stories complicate the picture of the Pharisees as simply Jesus's enemies?
- Jesus's sharpest warnings were aimed at devout, Bible-loving people. Where might Matthew 23:23-28 speak to our own church life today?

### [Second Temple Judaism](../articles/background-and-culture/second-temple-judaism.md) — Group

Read: Ezra 6:14-18; Nehemiah 8:1-8; Luke 4:16-21; John 10:22-23; Acts 23:6-8; Galatians 1:13-14

Study questions:
- According to Nehemiah 8:1-8, what did Ezra and the Levites do with the Law of Moses, and how did the people respond?
- In Luke 4:16-21, what does Jesus do "as was his custom" on the Sabbath, and what does that tell us about his own Jewish practice?
- Acts 23:6-8 shows Pharisees and Sadducees disagreeing about the resurrection. What does that suggest about how varied Jewish belief was in Paul's day?
- Which familiar Gospel scenes (a synagogue reading, a Temple feast, a debate with Pharisees) do you now picture differently, and why might knowing Jesus's Jewish world deepen your reading of him?

## bible-basics

### [Biblical Canon](../articles/bible-basics/biblical-canon.md) — Concept

Read: Luke 24:44; 2 Peter 3:15-16; 2 Timothy 3:16

Study questions:
- In Luke 24:44, which three groups of writings does the risen Jesus say speak about him?
- How does 2 Peter 3:15-16 describe Paul's letters, and what does that suggest about how early Christians regarded them?
- Why might it matter that the canon was recognised gradually by the whole church, rather than chosen in a single meeting?
- If your Bible has fewer or more Old Testament books than a friend's from another tradition, how could you talk about the difference with charity and accuracy?

## events-and-timeline

### [The Exodus](../articles/events-and-timeline/the-exodus.md) — Event

Read: Exodus 1:8-14; Exodus 3:7-15; Exodus 12:1-14; Exodus 12:26-27; Exodus 12:37-42; Exodus 12:51; Exodus 14:21-31; Exodus 15:1-21; Exodus 20:2; 1 Kings 6:1; Luke 9:30-31; 1 Corinthians 5:7; 1 Corinthians 10:1-11

Study questions:
- In Exodus 3:7-8, what four things does God say he has seen, heard, known and come down to do for his people?
- According to Exodus 12:26-27, what are parents to tell their children when they ask about the Passover, and why might Israel have built a question into the ritual?
- How does Exodus 20:2 connect the commandments to the rescue from Egypt? What difference does it make that God saves first and then gives the law?
- Paul calls Christ "our Passover" (1 Corinthians 5:7) and says the wilderness events "happened to them by way of example" (1 Corinthians 10:11). Where do you see your own story in Israel's — both the rescue and the grumbling afterwards?

## interpretation

### [Reading in Context](../articles/interpretation/reading-in-context.md) — Method

Read: Jeremiah 29:1-14; Philippians 4:10-14; Revelation 3:14-22; Nehemiah 8:8

1. Read the whole paragraph, and then the whole chapter, around the verse before deciding what it means.
2. Ask who is speaking, to whom, and in what situation; note what the book itself tells you about its author and first readers.
3. Trace the flow of thought by asking what comes just before, what comes just after, and how the verse fits the argument or story.
4. Identify the kind of writing (letter, poem, prophecy, narrative) and read it the way that kind of writing is meant to be read.
5. Use a study Bible or Bible dictionary to fill in historical and cultural background, without letting the background drown out the text.
6. Compare the passage with other Scripture on the same subject, especially clearer passages, and see how it fits the whole story that centres on Christ.
7. Only then ask what the passage means for you or your church, applying it in a way its first readers would recognise as faithful to it.

*A friend facing a job interview posts Jeremiah 29:11 as a guarantee that everything will go well.*

| Before | After |
|---|---|
| God has promised me success and no setbacks, so this interview is bound to work out. | God told exiles in Babylon to settle in and seek the city's peace, promising a future after seventy years; I can trust his good purposes and be faithful where I am, even if this interview fails. |

**Why:** Reading the whole letter (Jeremiah 29:1-14) shows a promise of long-range hope to a community in exile, which still comforts believers today but not as a pledge of quick personal success.

Study questions:
- According to Jeremiah 29:1-10, who first received the letter containing verse 11, where were they, and how long did God say they would wait?
- Read Philippians 4:10-14. What "all things" is Paul talking about in verse 13, and how does that change the way the verse is often used?
- Revelation 3:20 is often used in evangelism. To whom is it first addressed (Revelation 3:14-22), and what does that add to its meaning?
- Which verse do you hold dear but have never read in its surrounding chapter, and what do you notice when you read the whole chapter this week?

## life-and-teachings-of-jesus

### [Sermon on the Mount](../articles/life-and-teachings-of-jesus/sermon-on-the-mount.md) — Passage

Read: Matthew 4:23-25; Matthew 5:1-20; Matthew 5:21-48; Matthew 6:1-34; Matthew 7:1-29; Matthew 8:1; Luke 6:17-49; Exodus 20:13-14

Study questions:
- Who is listening when Jesus begins to teach in Matthew 5:1-2, and who is listening when he finishes in Matthew 7:28-29? Why might both groups matter?
- In Matthew 5:17-20, what does Jesus say he came to do with the Law and the Prophets?
- Read the three examples of "secret" devotion in Matthew 6:1-18 (giving, prayer, fasting). What do they have in common, and which one most challenges your own habits?
- The sermon ends with two builders (Matthew 7:24-27). What is the difference between them, and what would "doing" one of these teachings look like for you or your group this week?

## new-testament-books

### [Acts of the Apostles](../articles/new-testament-books/acts-of-the-apostles.md) — Book

Read: Acts 1:1-3; Acts 1:8; Acts 1:9-11; Acts 2:1-4; Acts 2:41-42; Acts 7:58; Acts 8:5; Acts 9:3-4; Acts 10:44-45; Acts 11:26; Acts 13:2-3; Acts 15:1-6; Acts 15:28; Acts 16:9-10; Acts 28:14-16; Acts 28:30-31; Luke 1:1-4; Colossians 4:14

Study questions:
- Compare Luke 1:1-4 with Acts 1:1-3. Who is addressed in both, and what does Acts say the "former treatise" was about?
- Acts 1:8 names Jerusalem, Judea and Samaria, and "the uttermost part of the earth." Trace where the story is in chapters 2, 8, 10 and 28. How does the book follow that map?
- What question brought the apostles and elders together in Acts 15:1-6, and how does the phrase "it seemed good to the Holy Spirit, and to us" (15:28) describe the way they reached a decision?
- Acts ends with Paul under guard in Rome yet preaching "with all boldness, none forbidding him" (28:31). Why might Luke end there rather than with Paul's trial, and what does that say about the ongoing story the church still lives in?

### [Gospel of Matthew](../articles/new-testament-books/gospel-of-matthew.md) — Book

Read: Matthew 1:1; Matthew 1:22-23; Matthew 4:17; Matthew 5:17-20; Matthew 7:28-29; Matthew 9:9; Matthew 16:16-18; Matthew 18:17-20; Matthew 28:18-20

Study questions:
- Matthew 1:1 calls Jesus "the son of David, the son of Abraham." Why might Matthew open with those two names in particular?
- Find the sentence that closes each of the five teaching blocks (Matthew 7:28, 11:1, 13:53, 19:1 and 26:1). What does each discourse teach disciples about life in the kingdom?
- In Matthew 5:17-20, what does Jesus say he came to do with the Law and the Prophets, and what kind of righteousness does he ask of his followers?
- The Gospel begins with "God with us" (1:23) and ends with "I am with you always" (28:20). How does that promise frame everything in between, and where do you need to hear it this week?

## new-testament-people

### [Paul](../articles/new-testament-people/paul.md) — Person

Read: Acts 8:1-3; Acts 13:1-4; Acts 13:47; Acts 9:1-30; Acts 13:9; Acts 18:3; Acts 22:3; Acts 22:25-28; Acts 28:30-31; Galatians 1:15-24; Philippians 3:4-14; 1 Corinthians 15:8-10; 2 Corinthians 11:25

Study questions:
- In Acts 9:1-6, what is Saul doing when Jesus meets him, and what question does Jesus ask him?
- Compare Acts 9:20-30 with Galatians 1:15-24. What does each account emphasise about what Paul did after his conversion?
- In Philippians 3:4-8, which credentials does Paul list, and how does he now value them?
- Paul calls himself "the least of the apostles" yet says God's grace was not in vain (1 Corinthians 15:9-10). How might his story shape the way we see people who have done harm, or our own past?
