# Moses

Moses is the towering human figure of the Old Testament: the reluctant shepherd God sent to Pharaoh, the leader of the Exodus, the mediator of the covenant at Sinai and the intercessor who carried a complaining people through the wilderness.[^s2][^s16] Deuteronomy ends by saying no prophet like him had arisen in Israel, one whom the LORD knew face to face.[^s10] Jews honour him as *Moshe Rabbenu*, "Moses our Teacher," and Christians read him as a faithful servant whose life points ahead to Christ.[^s19][^s14][^s17]

## Why it matters

Exodus through Deuteronomy revolve around Moses, and the law given through him shaped Israel's worship, ethics and life together.[^s18][^s16] The New Testament keeps returning to him: at the Transfiguration, in Stephen's speech, in Hebrews' roll of faith and in John's prologue.[^s11][^s13][^s15][^s12]

## Life and story

**Drawn out of the water.** Born to Levite parents under Pharaoh's oppression, Moses was hidden for three months, then set in a papyrus basket among the reeds while his sister watched. Pharaoh's daughter found him, hired his own mother to nurse him, and named him Moses, "because I drew him out of the water" (Exodus 2:1-10, WEB).[^s1] Exodus 6:20 names his parents as Amram and Jochebed and his brother as Aaron; Miriam is traditionally identified as the sister by the river.[^s4][^s17] Stephen adds that he "was instructed in all the wisdom of the Egyptians" (Acts 7:22, WEB).[^s13]

**Fugitive in Midian.** As a grown man Moses killed an Egyptian who was beating a Hebrew, then fled to Midian, where he married Zipporah, daughter of the priest Reuel, and named his son Gershom: "I have lived as a foreigner in a foreign land" (Exodus 2:11-22, WEB).[^s1]

**The burning bush.** Near Horeb, God spoke from a bush that burned without being consumed: he had seen his people's affliction and was sending Moses to Pharaoh. To Moses' "Who am I?" God promised his presence and revealed his name, "I AM WHO I AM" (Exodus 3:11-14, WEB).[^s2] When Moses pleaded that he was "slow of speech" (Exodus 4:10, WEB), God gave him Aaron as spokesman.[^s3]

**Exodus and Sinai.** Through the plagues and the first Passover God brought Israel out of Egypt, and at Sinai Moses received the Ten Commandments and the covenant law that bound the tribes into one people.[^s16] When the people made a golden calf, Moses broke the tablets — yet pleaded for them, even asking God to "blot me out of your book" if they could not be forgiven (Exodus 32:11-14, 32:19, 32:32, WEB).[^s5] "Yahweh spoke to Moses face to face, as a man speaks to his friend" (Exodus 33:11, WEB), yet he was shown only God's goodness passing by, since no one may see God's face and live.[^s6]

**Wilderness and death.** When Miriam and Aaron challenged him, the narrator calls Moses very humble, and God defended the servant he spoke with "mouth to mouth" (Numbers 12:1-8).[^s7] At Meribah, told to speak to the rock, Moses called the people "rebels" and struck it twice; because he did not trust God, he would not lead the assembly into the land (Numbers 20:7-12).[^s8] Aged 120, he saw the land from Mount Nebo and died there; no one knows his grave, and Joshua, on whom he had laid hands, led Israel on (Deuteronomy 34:1-9).[^s10]

## Legacy in Scripture

Moses promised that God would raise up "a prophet ... like me" (Deuteronomy 18:15, WEB), a pattern for Israel's prophets that Christians read as pointing to Jesus.[^s9][^s16][^s17] At the Transfiguration "Moses and Elijah appeared" talking with Jesus (Matthew 17:3, WEB), often read as the law and the prophets bearing witness to him.[^s11][^s16] Hebrews praises Moses' choice to suffer with God's people, and calls him faithful "as a servant" in God's house, while Christ is faithful "as a Son" (Hebrews 11:24-26; 3:5-6, WEB).[^s15][^s14] John writes, "For the law was given through Moses. Grace and truth came through Jesus Christ" (John 1:17, WEB).[^s12]

## Views across traditions

All Christian traditions honour Moses as prophet, lawgiver and intercessor.[^s16][^s17][^s20] The Orthodox Church keeps 4 September for the "Holy Prophet and God-seer Moses" and links the unburnt bush to the Virgin Mary's conception of Christ.[^s20] The *Catholic Encyclopedia* presents Moses as a type of Christ, whom it calls the "incomparable Antitype."[^s17] In Judaism he is the greatest of the prophets; Maimonides made the unique level of his prophecy one of his thirteen principles of faith.[^s19]

## Scholarly views

Nearly everything known about Moses comes from Exodus through Deuteronomy; no inscription outside the Bible names him.[^s16][^s18] Tradition regarded him as the Torah's author, and many believers still hold Mosaic authorship; many critical scholars see the Pentateuch as built from several sources over time, so that Moses can be recovered only in broad outline.[^s18][^s19][^s20] Martin Noth doubted much of the traditional picture; R. K. Harrison argues such a central figure could hardly have been invented.[^s18][^s16] *Britannica* dates the Exodus around 1290 BC, later than the fifteenth-century date some derive from biblical chronology.[^s18] Even the name is debated: Exodus links it to a Hebrew verb "to draw out," while many Egyptologists connect it with an Egyptian word for "child."[^s1][^s17]

## Common misreadings

Moses is not a flawless hero: he killed a man, begged God to send someone else, and lost his entry into the land through distrust at Meribah.[^s1][^s3][^s8] His greatness lies in God's faithfulness and his persistent intercession.[^s5][^s16] Nor should he be set against Jesus as "bad law" versus "good grace": Hebrews calls him faithful, and John presents law and grace as successive gifts of the same God.[^s14][^s12]

## Connections

Moses **was a member of** the Levites and the Israelites (Exodus 2:1-15).[^s1] He **was the brother of** Aaron (Exodus 6:20)[^s4] and **married** Zipporah (Exodus 2:15-22).[^s1] His work at Sinai **illustrates** the Mosaic covenant that bound the tribes into a nation.[^s16] Hebrews 11:23-29 makes him an example that **illustrates** faith.[^s15] Christian tradition reads him as one who **prefigures** Jesus, the prophet like Moses.[^s17][^s9] He is also tied to the Exodus, Miriam, Joshua, the Ten Commandments, the Pentateuch and the Transfiguration.[^s16][^s10][^s11][^s18]
