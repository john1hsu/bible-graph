# David

David is the shepherd from Bethlehem who became Israel's greatest king: giant-killer, court musician, fugitive, conqueror of Jerusalem and the man to whom God promised a throne for ever.[^s1][^s3][^s7][^s17] Scripture tells his story with unusual candour, including his adultery with Bathsheba and the death of her husband Uriah, and his repentance when the prophet Nathan confronted him.[^s8][^s20] The New Testament opens by calling Jesus "the son of David" (Matthew 1:1, WEB).[^s11]

## Why it matters

More of the Old Testament is devoted to David than to almost any other figure: his rise and reign fill most of 1 and 2 Samuel, his death opens 1 Kings, and many psalms carry his name.[^s17][^s20] Above all, God's covenant with him in 2 Samuel 7 shaped Israel's hope for a coming king, a hope the Gospels and Paul see fulfilled in Jesus, who is born "of the offspring of David according to the flesh" (Romans 1:3, WEB).[^s7][^s14][^s19]

## Life and story

**Anointed in Bethlehem.** When Saul disobeyed, Samuel told him that the LORD had sought "a man after his own heart" (1 Samuel 13:14, WEB).[^s2] God then sent Samuel to Jesse of Bethlehem, among whose sons he had "provided a king" (16:1, WEB).[^s1] Jesse presented seven sons, but Samuel was told that "man looks at the outward appearance, but Yahweh looks at the heart" (1 Samuel 16:7, WEB). The youngest, out keeping the sheep, was anointed, and "Yahweh's Spirit came mightily on David from that day forward" (16:11-13).[^s1] He entered Saul's service as a harpist whose playing soothed the troubled king, and became his armour-bearer (16:14-23).[^s1]

**Goliath.** Facing the Philistine champion, about "six cubits and a span" tall (17:4), David said he came "in the name of Yahweh of Armies" and that "the battle is Yahweh's" (17:45-47, WEB). He felled Goliath with a sling and a stone, with "no sword in David's hand" (17:49-50).[^s3]

**Jonathan, Michal and Saul's jealousy.** Saul's son Jonathan loved David, and the two made a covenant (18:1-3); Saul gave him his daughter Michal as wife (18:27).[^s4] But when the women sang that Saul had slain thousands and David "his ten thousands" (18:7, WEB), Saul's jealousy drove David into years as an outlaw.[^s4][^s17] In a cave David secretly cut off the edge of Saul's robe, then refused to harm "Yahweh's anointed" (24:4-6).[^s5] When Saul and Jonathan fell in battle, David mourned Jonathan, whose love had been "wonderful" (2 Samuel 1:26, WEB).[^s6]

**King in Hebron and Jerusalem.** The elders of Israel anointed David king at Hebron; he was thirty and reigned forty years, seven and a half over Judah in Hebron and thirty-three over all Israel and Judah (2 Samuel 5:3-5).[^s6][^s10] He took "the stronghold of Zion," which became "the city of David" (5:7, WEB), and brought the ark there, dancing "before Yahweh with all his might" while Michal despised him (6:14-16).[^s6]

**The promise.** When David wished to build a house for the ark, God replied through Nathan that he would make David a house instead: his offspring would build the temple, and "your throne will be established forever" (2 Samuel 7:1-16, WEB).[^s7]

**Sin and repentance.** At the height of his power David took Bathsheba, wife of Uriah the Hittite, and had Uriah set "in the forefront of the hottest battle" (2 Samuel 11:1-15, WEB).[^s6] Nathan's parable of a rich man who stole a poor man's lamb ended with "You are the man!" David confessed, "I have sinned against Yahweh," but the child died (12:7-14).[^s8] Psalm 51, headed as David's prayer after Nathan came to him, asks, "Create in me a clean heart, O God" (51:10, WEB).[^s9] Bathsheba later bore Solomon (12:24),[^s8] but from then on violence and sexual sin troubled David's own household.[^s20] David died after reigning forty years and was buried in "David's city" (1 Kings 2:10-11, WEB).[^s10]

## Legacy in Scripture

Matthew's genealogy runs through "King David," who fathered Solomon "by her who had been Uriah's wife" (Matthew 1:6, WEB).[^s11] Gabriel promises Mary that God will give her son "the throne of his father David" (Luke 1:32, WEB), and Paul preaches that from David's offspring God has brought Israel a Saviour (Acts 13:22-23).[^s12][^s13] The Catholic Encyclopedia calls David a type of the Messiah and "the model of penitents," and Christians have long read the shepherd king of Bethlehem as pointing to Christ the Good Shepherd.[^s15] In Jewish tradition, too, the restoration of the house of David is tied to the hope of the messianic age.[^s19]

## Scholarly views

Tradition and the biblical chronology place David's reign around 1000 BC.[^s15][^s17] The Tel Dan Stele, found in northern Israel in 1993, records an Aramean king's victory over the "house of David," and most scholars accept it as the earliest reference to David outside the Bible.[^s18][^s19] Scholars still disagree about the size of his kingdom: some, such as Israel Finkelstein, see a modest chiefdom, and *Britannica* notes the view that David was a pastoral tribal leader, while others defend a larger kingdom closer to the biblical picture.[^s18][^s17] The older *Jewish Encyclopedia* judged a strictly historical account of his youth impossible, while still crediting him with uniting the tribes.[^s16] Psalm superscriptions name David often, but scholars debate how many psalms he actually wrote.[^s16][^s20]

## Common misreadings

David is not a flawless hero, nor is "a man after God's own heart" a reward for good behaviour: the same narrative records adultery and murder.[^s2][^s6][^s8] His example lies in his trust in God and his honest repentance.[^s9][^s15]

## Connections

David **was one of** the Israelites, a son of Jesse of Bethlehem in Judah (1 Samuel 17:12).[^s3] He **succeeded** Saul, whom God removed to raise up David as king (Acts 13:22).[^s13] He **married** Michal, Saul's daughter (1 Samuel 18:27),[^s4] and Bathsheba, and **was the father of** Solomon (Matthew 1:6).[^s11] His life **illustrates** the Davidic covenant of 2 Samuel 7.[^s7] Christian tradition reads him as one who **prefigures** Jesus.[^s15] His dynasty **is attested outside the Bible by** the Tel Dan Stele.[^s18] He is also tied to Jerusalem, Bethlehem, the Psalms, 1 Kings and the Temple.[^s6][^s10][^s17][^s20]
