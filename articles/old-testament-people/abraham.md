# Abraham

Abraham is where the story of Israel begins. In Genesis 12 God calls one man, Abram, to leave home on nothing but his word, promising that "all the families of the earth will be blessed through you" (Genesis 12:3, WEB).[^s2] Jews honour him as the ancestor of Israel and a pioneer of faith; Christians read him, with Paul, as "the father of us all" who believe (Romans 4:16, WEB); and Muslims also honour him.[^s13][^s20]

## Why it matters

Abraham's story (Genesis 11:26–25:10) launches the covenant the rest of the Bible unfolds: a people, a land and a blessing for the nations.[^s17][^s18] The New Testament opens with Jesus as "the son of Abraham" (Matthew 1:1, WEB), and Paul, Hebrews and James all use him to explain faith.[^s11][^s13][^s15][^s16]

## Life and story

**From Ur to Canaan.** Abram, son of Terah, married Sarai, who "was barren" (Genesis 11:29-30, WEB); Terah led the family from "Ur of the Chaldees" toward Canaan but died in Haran (11:31-32).[^s1] There God said, "Leave your country, and your relatives, and your father's house," promising a great nation, a great name and blessing; "so Abram went," aged seventy-five, with Sarai and his nephew Lot (12:1-5, WEB).[^s2] At Shechem God promised the land to his offspring, and Abram built an altar (12:6-7).[^s2]

**Fears and shortcuts.** In a famine Abram went to Egypt and, afraid for his life, passed Sarai off as his sister; God protected her, and Pharaoh sent Abram away (12:10-20).[^s2] Still childless, he heard God say, "Count the stars"; "He believed in the LORD, who credited it to him for righteousness," and God bound himself by covenant to give the land (15:5-6, 18, WEBBE).[^s3] When years passed, Sarai gave him her Egyptian servant Hagar, who bore Ishmael when Abram was eighty-six (16:1-16).[^s4]

**A new name.** At ninety-nine Abram heard, "Walk before me, and be blameless"; his name became Abraham, "for I have made you the father of a multitude of nations," circumcision became "a token of the covenant," and Sarai became Sarah (17:1-15, WEB).[^s5] Abraham laughed at the thought of a son at a hundred; God named the child Isaac (17:17-19).[^s5] At Mamre he welcomed three visitors and heard, "Is anything too hard for Yahweh?" (18:14, WEB); then he pleaded for Sodom: "Shouldn't the Judge of all the earth do right?" (18:25, WEB).[^s6]

**Isaac.** Isaac was born when Abraham was a hundred, and Sarah said, "God has made me laugh" (21:5-6, WEB).[^s7] Abraham grieved when Hagar and Ishmael were sent away, but God promised Ishmael a nation too (21:9-18).[^s7] Then "God tested Abraham": "take your son, your only son, whom you love, even Isaac," to Moriah (22:1-2, WEB). On the way Abraham told Isaac, "God will provide himself the lamb"; an angel stopped his hand, a ram was caught in a thicket, and he named the place "Yahweh Will Provide" (22:8-14, WEB).[^s8] He died at 175, "full of years," and Isaac and Ishmael buried him with Sarah in the cave of Machpelah (25:7-10, WEB).[^s9]

## Legacy in Scripture

Isaiah calls Israel "the seed of Abraham my friend" (Isaiah 41:8, ASV), and James adds that "he was called the friend of God" (James 2:23, WEB).[^s10][^s16] Jesus told those claiming Abraham as father to "do the works of Abraham," and said, "before Abraham came into existence, I AM" (John 8:39, 58, WEB).[^s12]

Paul builds Romans 4 on Genesis 15:6: righteousness was credited to Abraham before he was circumcised, "that he might be the father of all those who believe" (Romans 4:11, WEB).[^s13] In Galatians, "those who are of faith, the same are children of Abraham," and Christ is the promised "seed" (Galatians 3:7, 16, 29, WEB).[^s14] Hebrews praises his going out "not knowing where he went" and reasons that he trusted "God is able to raise up even from the dead" (Hebrews 11:8, 19, WEB).[^s15] James points to the offering of Isaac to show that "faith worked with his works" (James 2:21-22, WEB).[^s16]

## Views across traditions

All Christian traditions honour Abraham as the model of faith.[^s17][^s23][^s25] The *Catechism of the Catholic Church* presents him as the model of the obedience of faith and the "father of all who believe," and reads his readiness to offer his son as conformed to the Father who "will not spare his own Son" (paras. 144-146, 2572).[^s23][^s24] The Orthodox Church keeps 9 October for the "Righteous Forefather Abraham and his nephew Lot."[^s25] Evangelical Protestants stress, with Paul, that the blessing belongs to all who share Abraham's faith.[^s17] Classical Jewish interpreters pictured him recognising the one God even before his call.[^s20]

## Scholarly views

Genesis is essentially the only source for Abraham; no ancient inscription clearly names him.[^s18][^s21] Many mid-twentieth-century scholars placed him early in the second millennium BC; André Parrot dated the departure from Ur to about 2000 BC.[^s22][^s19] In the 1970s Thomas L. Thompson and John Van Seters argued the stories are later literary creations; Martin Selman replied by defending careful use of ancient parallels.[^s22] P. Kyle McCarter thinks Abraham may have been a real individual whose period cannot be fixed, though family names around Haran fit second-millennium Amorite culture.[^s21] Bible Odyssey concludes that historians do not know whether he existed and that the stories mix memory and legend.[^s20]

## Common misreadings

Abraham is no spotless hero: the narrator does not hide his lie about Sarah or the shortcut with Hagar.[^s2][^s4] His greatness lies in God's faithfulness and a trust that grew over decades.[^s17] Nor do Paul and James contradict each other: Paul appeals to the faith of Genesis 15, James to that same faith proved in Genesis 22, and both quote Genesis 15:6.[^s13][^s16][^s18]

## Connections

Abraham **was married to** Sarah (Genesis 11:29)[^s1] and **was the father of** Ishmael (Genesis 16:15)[^s4] and Isaac (Genesis 21:3).[^s7] His story **illustrates** the Abrahamic covenant of land, offspring and blessing.[^s17] Hebrews 11:8-19 makes him an example that **illustrates** faith,[^s15] and Romans 4 has his credited righteousness **illustrate** justification by faith.[^s13] He is also tied to Lot, Hagar, the binding of Isaac and Matthew's genealogy.[^s1][^s8][^s11]
