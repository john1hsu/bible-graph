# Money and Generosity

Money touches daily life constantly, and Scripture speaks about it plainly. It treats wealth as part of God's good gifts, warns that it can quietly take God's place, and calls God's people to an open hand toward those in need.[^s13]

## Why it matters

Jesus put it starkly: "You can't serve both God and Mammon" (Matthew 6:24, World English Bible).[^s1] As Craig Blomberg notes, Jesus treats wealth as a rival master and a deceiver, so how Christians use money shows what they trust.[^s13] Christians across traditions keep generosity at the centre of discipleship: the *Catechism of the Catholic Church* calls almsgiving a chief witness to fraternal charity and a work of justice pleasing to God, and John Wesley's famous sermon urged Methodists to earn and save so that they could give.[^s16][^s17]

## What Scripture says

**The law opens Israel's hand.** Deuteronomy commanded a release of debts every seventh year, and forbade Israelites to harden their hearts or "shut your hand from your poor brother"; instead, "you shall surely open your hand to him" (Deuteronomy 15:7-8, WEB).[^s10] Giving is to be without a grudging heart, because the poor will never cease from the land (Deuteronomy 15:9-11).[^s10] The law also limited interest, left gleanings for the poor and set aside a third-year tithe for the Levite, the foreigner, the orphan and the widow.[^s13][^s14] The *Jewish Encyclopedia* notes that the Hebrew word for charity, *tzedakah*, means "righteousness": giving to the poor was justice owed, and later Jewish teachers prized discreet giving that protects the receiver's dignity.[^s15]

**Wisdom asks for enough.** One proverb prays, "give me neither poverty nor riches," lest wealth make the speaker forget God or poverty tempt him to steal (Proverbs 30:8-9, King James Version).[^s12] Blomberg observes that the Old Testament often sees wealth as blessing, yet refuses to equate riches with righteousness, and the prophets condemn wealth gained by crushing the poor.[^s13]

**The tithe.** Abraham and Jacob gave a tenth before the law, which then assigned tithes to the Levites and, in the third year, to the needy (Numbers 18; Deuteronomy 14).[^s14] Through Malachi, God charged his people with robbing him "in tithes and offerings" and told them to bring the whole tithe into the storehouse (Malachi 3:8-10, WEB).[^s11] Churches differ on how the tithe applies to Christians. The *Catechism* lists tithing among the Old Testament laws that express the call to open one's hand to the needy, while Wesley pressed believers beyond any fixed tenth, since everything they hold is entrusted to them by God.[^s16][^s17]

**Jesus warns and invites.** Asked to settle an inheritance dispute, Jesus warned against covetousness, "for a man's life doesn't consist of the abundance of the things which he possesses" (Luke 12:15, WEB), and told of a rich man who built bigger barns but was not "rich toward God" (Luke 12:16-21).[^s2] He told his disciples to give in secret rather than to be seen, and to store up treasure in heaven rather than on earth (Matthew 6:1-4, 19-21), and to seek God's kingdom first instead of worrying (Matthew 6:25-34).[^s1] He loved a rich man who asked about eternal life and called him to sell what he had, give to the poor and follow; the man went away sorrowful (Mark 10:17-22).[^s4] Blomberg cautions against assuming this call is given to every disciple: Zacchaeus gave half his goods to the poor and promised fourfold repayment to anyone he had cheated, and Jesus declared that salvation had come to his house (Luke 19:8-9).[^s13][^s5] Jesus also praised a poor widow whose two small coins were "more than all," since she gave out of her poverty (Mark 12:41-44).[^s3]

**The church learns to share.** In Jerusalem, landowners sold property so that no believer was in need, and Barnabas laid the price of a field at the apostles' feet (Acts 4:32-37).[^s6] Paul urged the Corinthians to give to a collection for needy believers, pointing to the Macedonians, generous in deep poverty, and above all to Christ, who "though he was rich, yet for your sakes he became poor" (2 Corinthians 8:1-9, WEB).[^s7] Gifts are measured by what one has, not by what one lacks, so that abundance meets need (2 Corinthians 8:12-15), and each should give as decided in the heart, "for God loves a cheerful giver" (2 Corinthians 9:7, WEB).[^s7][^s8]

## Pitfalls

**Misquoting 1 Timothy.** A common saying claims that money is the root of all evil. The text says something sharper: "the love of money is a root of all kinds of evil" (1 Timothy 6:10, WEB).[^s9] Money itself is not condemned; the same chapter tells the rich not to trust uncertain riches but to be "rich in good works," ready to share (1 Timothy 6:17-18).[^s9]

**Treating giving as an investment scheme.** Malachi 3:10 and the sowing image of 2 Corinthians 9:6 are sometimes read as a guaranteed financial return. Paul's point is that God supplies enough so that his people can "abound to every good work" (2 Corinthians 9:8, WEB), and the letter to Timothy commends "godliness with contentment" (1 Timothy 6:6, WEB).[^s8][^s9]

**Generosity as display.** Jesus warns against sounding a trumpet over one's giving (Matthew 6:2).[^s1]

**Guilt or despair instead of grace.** Paul asks for willing giving, "not grudgingly or under compulsion" (2 Corinthians 9:7, WEB), measured by what one actually has (2 Corinthians 8:12).[^s8][^s7] This article is not financial advice: if debt or money worries weigh heavily on you, talk with a pastor and a qualified financial or debt counsellor, and if the stress becomes overwhelming, see a doctor or counsellor.

- **Sermon on the Mount** (`supportedBy`): Matthew 6 on secret giving, heavenly treasure and God and Mammon.[^s1]
- **Parable of the Rich Fool** (`supportedBy`): Luke 12:13-21 warns against covetousness.[^s2]
- **Deuteronomy** (`supportedBy`): Deuteronomy 15 commands the release of debts and an open hand to the poor.[^s10]
- **The widow's offering** (`supportedBy`): Mark 12:41-44 measures a gift by its cost, not its size.[^s3]
