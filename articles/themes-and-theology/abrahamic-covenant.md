# Abrahamic Covenant

The Abrahamic covenant is the solemn commitment God makes to Abraham (first called Abram) in Genesis 12-22. It promises a people, a land, and a blessing reaching beyond his family, since "all of the families of the earth will be blessed in you" (Genesis 12:3, WEB).[^s1] A covenant rite (Genesis 15), a sign (Genesis 17) and an oath (Genesis 22) then seal them; such binding agreements were often sealed with blood in the ancient world.[^s2][^s3][^s4][^s14]

## Why it matters

The Exodus begins when God "remembered his covenant with Abraham" (Exodus 2:24, WEB), and Luke's account of Jesus's birth recalls "the oath which he swore to Abraham our father" (Luke 1:73, WEB).[^s7][^s9] Paul grounds his teaching on faith and grace in Abraham, "the father of us all" (Romans 4:16, WEB), and the gospel's claim that Gentiles are blessed in Christ only makes sense against what was promised to him.[^s10][^s12]

## Biblical basis

**The call (Genesis 12).** God tells Abram to leave his country for a land he will be shown, and promises to make him a great nation, to bless him and to make him a blessing to every family on earth. When Abram reaches Canaan, God adds, "I will give this land to your seed" (12:7, WEB).[^s1]

**The covenant ceremony (Genesis 15).** Childless Abram is shown the stars, and "he believed in Yahweh; and he reckoned it to him for righteousness" (15:6, WEB).[^s2] Asking how he will know he will inherit the land, he is told to cut animals in half and lay the halves opposite each other; after dark a smoking furnace and a flaming torch pass between the pieces, and God covenants to give his offspring the land from the river of Egypt to the Euphrates (15:9-18).[^s2] In the similar rite of Jeremiah 34:18, the parties walked between the halves of a calf, invoking that fate on themselves if they broke faith.[^s8] Here only God's fire passes through; Kyle Dunham, building on Moshe Weinfeld's work on royal grants, reads this as God alone taking responsibility for the promise.[^s15]

**The sign (Genesis 17).** When Abram is ninety-nine, God renames him Abraham, "father of a multitude of nations", and calls the covenant "everlasting": God will be God to Abraham and his descendants and give them "all the land of Canaan" (17:5-8, WEB).[^s3] Circumcision of every male, on the eighth day, becomes "a token of the covenant" (17:10-12, WEB).[^s3] Sarai becomes Sarah, and the covenant is to continue through her son Isaac, though Ishmael is also blessed (17:15-21).[^s3]

**The oath (Genesis 22).** After Abraham's willingness to offer Isaac, God swears by himself to bless him, to multiply his offspring "like the stars of the heavens" and to bless all nations through his seed (22:15-18, WEB).[^s4]

## Through the Bible

God renews the same promises to Isaac, confirming "the oath which I swore to Abraham your father" (Genesis 26:3, WEB), and to Jacob at Bethel (Genesis 28:13-15).[^s5][^s6] When Israel cries out in Egypt, God's memory of this covenant sets the Exodus in motion (Exodus 2:23-25).[^s7]

In the New Testament, Zechariah praises God for remembering his oath to Abraham (Luke 1:72-73).[^s9] Paul points out that Abraham was counted righteous *before* he was circumcised, so circumcision was "a seal of the righteousness of the faith" he already had, making him father of all who believe (Romans 4:9-11, WEB).[^s10] Galatians says the Good News was preached beforehand to Abraham, reads the promised "seed" as Christ, and argues that the law given 430 years later cannot cancel the promise; all who belong to Christ are "heirs according to promise" (Galatians 3:8, 16-18, 29, WEB).[^s12] Hebrews adds that God's oath gives believers "strong encouragement" (Hebrews 6:13-18, WEB).[^s13] Yet God has not rejected Israel, which remains "beloved for the fathers' sake", since God's gifts and calling are irrevocable (Romans 11:1, 28-29, WEB).[^s11]

## Views across traditions

Christians agree that the covenant with Abraham is fulfilled in Jesus Christ and that believers share Abraham's faith and blessing; they differ on how its sign and its promises to Israel, especially the land, carry forward.[^s10][^s15][^s21]

- **Catholic.** The *Catechism* teaches that Abraham's promised progeny, in whom all nations are blessed, is Christ himself, through whom the Spirit gathers God's scattered children (CCC 706).[^s16] It also holds that the covenants and promises belong to the Jewish people, whose calling, citing Romans 11:29, is irrevocable (CCC 839).[^s17]
- **Eastern Orthodox.** On the Sunday of the Forefathers, in mid-December before the Nativity, the Orthodox Church honours Christ's ancestors according to the flesh, especially Abraham, and links the promise that all nations will be blessed in his seed to Christ's coming.[^s20]
- **Reformed (covenant theology).** The Westminster Confession treats the promises, sacrifices and circumcision given to Israel as one administration of a single "covenant of grace" that points to Christ, now administered through Word and sacraments.[^s18] The Heidelberg Catechism therefore teaches that the children of believers belong to the covenant and should be baptized, baptism having taken the place of circumcision (Q&A 74).[^s19]
- **Dispensational.** Dispensational teachers at Dallas Theological Seminary hold that Israel remains in God's plan to the end and is not replaced by the church, which has its own distinct calling; progressive dispensationalists see more continuity between the two.[^s21] Dispensationalists have usually called the covenant unconditional and covenant theologians have often stressed its conditions; Dunham proposes a unilateral, irrevocable royal grant that still carries real expectations.[^s15]

**A Jewish reading.** The covenant is also central to Judaism: rabbinic literature calls circumcision the covenant of "Abraham our father", and Jewish tradition reflects on Genesis 15 as the "covenant between the pieces".[^s14]

## Connections

It is an **instance of** biblical **covenant**.[^s14] Paul **contrasts** it with the later **Mosaic covenant**, which cannot annul the promise, and reads it as fulfilled in Christ, so it **complements** the **New Covenant** (Galatians 3:16-18).[^s12] God's remembrance of it **leads to** **the Exodus**.[^s7] Its rite is **explained by** ancient Near Eastern treaty and royal-grant customs.[^s15] It is told in **Genesis**, tied to **Abraham**, **Sarah** and **Isaac** and sworn at the **Binding of Isaac**, and Romans 4 relates it to **faith**, **justification** and **grace**.[^s3][^s4][^s10]
