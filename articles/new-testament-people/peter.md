# Peter

Peter is the disciple the Gospels show us most fully: eager, bold, quick to speak, capable of great faith and painful failure. Born Simon, he was a fisherman whose brother Andrew brought him to Jesus with the news, "We have found the Messiah!" (John 1:41, WEB).[^s2] Jesus renamed him Cephas, Aramaic for "rock," rendered in Greek as *Petros* (John 1:42).[^s2][^s14] Paul usually calls him Cephas, and James calls him Simeon (Acts 15:14).[^s14][^s11]

## Why it matters

Peter is named first in every list of the Twelve, is the disciple mentioned most often in the Gospels, and leads the church in the first half of Acts.[^s14] His story is one of Scripture's clearest pictures of grace: the man who denied Jesus three times was restored and entrusted with Christ's flock.[^s5][^s6] And because churches read Matthew 16 differently, Peter stands at the centre of long conversations about authority and unity.[^s18][^s19][^s20]

## Life and story

**Fisherman and disciple.** John places Peter's home town at Bethsaida (John 1:44); Mark finds him and Andrew casting nets in the Sea of Galilee when Jesus says, "Come after me, and I will make you into fishers for men" (Mark 1:17, WEB).[^s2][^s3] Peter was married, and Jesus healed his mother-in-law at the brothers' house in Capernaum (Mark 1:29-31).[^s4][^s14] With James and John he formed an inner circle and often spoke for the group.[^s14]

**Confession and rebuke.** Near Caesarea Philippi Peter confessed Jesus as the Christ, the Son of the living God. Jesus called him blessed, said, "You are Peter, and on this rock I will build my assembly," and promised him the keys of the Kingdom (Matthew 16:15-19, WEB).[^s1] Yet when Jesus spoke of his coming death, Peter protested and was rebuked as a stumbling block (Matthew 16:21-23).[^s1]

**Denial and restoration.** Jesus warned Peter that Satan would sift the disciples like wheat, but that he had prayed for Peter's faith and that Peter, once he turned back, must strengthen his brothers (Luke 22:31-34).[^s5] That night Peter denied him three times; the rooster crowed, "The Lord turned and looked at Peter," and he wept bitterly (Luke 22:54-62, WEB).[^s5] The risen Christ appeared to Cephas before the Twelve (1 Corinthians 15:5).[^s7] By the lake Jesus asked him three times whether he loved him, gave him a shepherd's charge each time, hinted at the death by which Peter would glorify God, and said, "Follow me" (John 21:15-19).[^s6]

**Leader of the early church.** At Pentecost Peter stood with the eleven and preached, and about three thousand were baptized (Acts 2:14, 2:41).[^s8] A vision at Joppa and the gift of the Spirit to the Roman centurion Cornelius's household taught him that God shows no favouritism (Acts 10).[^s9] Freed from Herod's prison by an angel, he went to the praying church at the house of John Mark's mother and then left Jerusalem (Acts 12:1-17).[^s10] At the Jerusalem council he argued that Jews and Gentiles alike are saved through the grace of the Lord Jesus (Acts 15:7-11).[^s11]

## Legacy in Scripture

Paul says Peter was entrusted with the gospel for the circumcised and names Cephas among the "pillars" (Galatians 2:7-9); he also tells frankly of opposing Peter at Antioch when Peter withdrew from eating with Gentile believers (Galatians 2:11-14).[^s12] Two letters bear Peter's name; the first was written through Silvanus and sends greetings from the church "in Babylon" and from "Mark my son" (1 Peter 5:12-13, ASV).[^s13] Around AD 96 Clement of Rome recalled Peter's many trials and martyrdom, and later tradition says he was crucified head downward in Rome under Nero.[^s17][^s15][^s20]

## Views across traditions

All traditions honour Peter as first among the apostles, the confessor of Christ restored after his fall, and a martyr; Catholic and Orthodox calendars keep his feast with Paul's on 29 June.[^s15][^s20] They differ on what Matthew 16:18-19 means for the church after him.

- **Catholic.** The *Catechism* teaches that Christ made Simon alone the "rock" of his Church, gave him the keys and made him shepherd of the whole flock, an office continued by the bishops under the primacy of the Pope as Peter's successor (CCC 880-882).[^s18]
- **Eastern Orthodox.** The Orthodox Church in America honours Peter and Paul as leaders of the apostles; the patristic sermon it reads with their feast presents Peter as standing for the whole Church, which received the keys.[^s20]
- **Lutheran.** The *Treatise on the Power and Primacy of the Pope* reads "this rock" as the ministry of Peter's confession and holds that the keys belong to the whole Church.[^s19]
- **Other Protestants** variously take the rock to be Christ, Peter's confession, or (as Gregg Allison argues, noting the Aramaic wordplay) Peter himself as confessor, without a line of successors.[^s21][^s14]

## Scholarly views

The New Testament never places Peter in Rome, but *Britannica* calls Clement's letter the strongest evidence that he was martyred there, and many scholars accept Rome under Nero as the setting.[^s16][^s17] Whether he founded the Roman church or was its first bishop is disputed, and excavations under St Peter's have not settled where he was buried.[^s16] *Britannica* treats his authorship of 1 Peter as an open question, and his authorship of 2 Peter is disputed.[^s16][^s14]

## Common misreadings

Some readers make the denial the defining fact about Peter, but Jesus prayed for him beforehand and restored him afterwards.[^s5][^s6] Popular jokes about Peter at the pearly gates go well beyond Matthew 16:19, which speaks of the keys of the Kingdom and of binding and loosing on earth.[^s1]

## Connections

Peter **was a member of** the apostles and **was the brother of** Andrew (John 1:40-41).[^s14][^s2] His restoration and his words at the council **illustrate** grace.[^s11] His martyrdom is **corroborated by** *1 Clement*.[^s17] He is also linked to Paul, Barnabas, John Mark, Pentecost, the Jerusalem council and the Gospel of Mark, which early tradition ties to his preaching.[^s12][^s10][^s15]
