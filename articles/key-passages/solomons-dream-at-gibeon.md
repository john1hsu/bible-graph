# Solomon's Dream at Gibeon

At the start of his reign the young King Solomon goes to Gibeon to worship, and there "Yahweh appeared to Solomon in a dream by night; and God said, 'Ask what I shall give you'" (1 Kings 3:5, WEB).[^s1] Solomon asks for "an understanding heart to judge your people" (3:9, WEB), and God, pleased, gives him wisdom and adds the riches and honour he did not request (3:10-13).[^s1] It opens the story of Solomon's reign.[^s12]

## Why it matters

Offered anything, a new ruler asks for the ability to listen and judge rightly for the sake of the people in his care.[^s1][^s11] The passage shows what God delights to give, and it quietly warns that a gift is not the same as faithfulness: the reign that opens so well ends under the judgement of 1 Kings 11.[^s4]

## Context

Chapter 3 opens with two notes of unease: he makes a marriage alliance with Pharaoh, and the people still sacrifice "in the high places, because there was no house built for the name of Yahweh" (3:1-2, WEB).[^s1] Solomon "loved Yahweh," yet "he sacrificed and burnt incense in the high places" (3:3, WEB). Gibeon was "the great high place," and he offered a thousand burnt offerings there (3:4).[^s1] The Chronicler adds that the Tent of Meeting Moses made in the wilderness stood at Gibeon (2 Chronicles 1:3).[^s3][^s12]

## Walking through the text

**God's open invitation (3:5).** The initiative is God's: in a dream by night he invites Solomon to ask.[^s1]

**Gratitude before asking (3:6-8).** Solomon first thanks God for his "great loving kindness" to David and for giving David a son on his throne (3:6, WEB).[^s1] Then he confesses his weakness: "I am but a little child. I don't know how to go out or come in" (3:7, WEB), set over a people too many to count (3:8).[^s1]

**The request (3:9).** He asks for an "understanding heart" to judge and to "discern between good and evil" (3:9, WEB).[^s1] The Hebrew is *lev shomea*, a heart that *hears*: *shomea* is a participle of *shama*, "to hear".[^s8] The United Bible Societies' translation notes explain that "hear" here also means "understand", and that "heart" means the mind and judgement rather than the emotions; renderings range from "understanding" to "attentive" or "obedient".[^s9][^s8]

**God's answer (3:10-14).** God is pleased that Solomon did not ask for long life, riches or "the life of your enemies," but for "understanding to discern justice" (3:11, WEB).[^s1] He gives a "wise and an understanding heart," and adds "that which you have not asked, both riches and honor" (3:12-13, WEB). Long life is promised on a condition: "If you will walk in my ways ... as your father David walked" (3:14, WEB). Solomon wakes and worships before the ark in Jerusalem (3:15).[^s1]

**The gift in action (3:16-28).** Two women who were prostitutes each claim a living baby. When Solomon calls for a sword, the true mother's love gives her away, and all Israel sees "that the wisdom of God was in him, to do justice" (3:28, WEB).[^s1] Wisdom here is not cleverness for its own sake but the ability to rule justly.[^s12]

## Interpretation

**Wisdom for service.** Solomon asks for wisdom to govern God's people, not for his own advancement.[^s1][^s12] His later fame, wisdom "as the sand that is on the seashore" (4:29, WEB) that drew hearers from all the kings of the earth, rests on this gift (4:29-34).[^s2]

**A gift that still had to be kept.** The narrator later says the LORD was angry with Solomon because his heart turned away from the God "who had appeared to him twice" (1 Kings 11:9, WEB).[^s4] The Catholic Encyclopedia notes that his wives led him to other gods and his heavy exactions split the kingdom.[^s11] Gibeon shows what God gives; Solomon's later life shows that a hearing heart must keep listening.

**Jewish and Christian readings.** Rabbinic tradition praised Solomon for asking for nothing but wisdom, and remembered him as the wise king above all others.[^s10] The Book of Wisdom, a deuterocanonical book in the Catholic Old Testament that many Protestants do not count as Scripture, gives its own version of this prayer in chapter 9, asking God to send Wisdom and his holy spirit; the NABRE notes point back to 1 Kings 3:6-9 and 2 Chronicles 1:8-10.[^s7][^s14] Christians hear Solomon's choice echoed when Jesus says that those who "seek first God's Kingdom" will receive "all these things" as well (Matthew 6:33, WEB),[^s5] and in James's promise that anyone who lacks wisdom may "ask of God, who gives to all liberally" (James 1:5, WEB).[^s6]

**Ancient setting.** Ruth Fidler reads the chapter as a "royal novella", an ancient Near Eastern way of telling how a god approved a new king, and notes other kings' prayers for long life and victory over rivals: exactly what Solomon did not ask for.[^s13] The Jewish Encyclopedia judged that the picture of Solomon's wisdom owes much to the later historians who shaped Kings.[^s10]

**Common misreadings.** The passage is not a formula: ask for the "right" thing and riches will follow. The riches were God's free addition, and God never promised Solomon that wealth would keep his heart (3:13; 11:9).[^s1][^s4] Nor is it a lesson in seeking guidance through dreams; it tells what God did for one king at a turning point in Israel's history.[^s1][^s13]

## Connections

The dream is **part of** [1 Kings](bg:1-kings) and **features** [Solomon](bg:solomon) at [Gibeon](bg:gibeon).[^s1] It **parallels** the shorter account in [2 Chronicles](bg:2-chronicles) 1, where Solomon asks for "wisdom and knowledge" (1:10, WEB).[^s3] It **teaches** [wisdom](bg:wisdom) as God's gift for justice (3:9-12, 28),[^s1] and the [Davidic covenant](bg:davidic-covenant), since Solomon thanks God for setting David's son on his throne and is asked to walk as David walked (3:6, 14).[^s1] It **is explained by** its setting in the [ancient Near East](bg:ancient-near-east), where gods were said to confirm new kings in dreams.[^s13] It links to [the Temple](bg:the-temple) Solomon went on to build (3:1),[^s1] the [Book of Wisdom](bg:book-of-wisdom) among the [deuterocanonical books](bg:deuterocanonical-books),[^s7][^s14] and Jesus' words on seeking first the [kingdom of God](bg:kingdom-of-god) in the [Sermon on the Mount](bg:sermon-on-the-mount).[^s5]
