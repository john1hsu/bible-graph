# The Temple

For much of the biblical story, "the house of the LORD" in Jerusalem was the centre of Israel's worship. Two temples stood on the same hill: the First Temple, built by Solomon and burned by the Babylonians, and the Second Temple, rebuilt by returning exiles and later enlarged by Herod the Great, which stood in the days of Jesus and the apostles until Rome destroyed it in AD 70.[^s20]

## Why it matters

The Temple is the high point of God's promise to dwell among his people, which began with the tabernacle in the wilderness: "Let them make me a sanctuary, that I may dwell among them" (Exodus 25:8, WEB).[^s1] It is also bound up with God's covenant with David, whose son was to build "a house for my name" while God built David a lasting royal "house" (2 Samuel 7:11-13).[^s2] The New Testament then applies the Temple's language to Jesus, the church and the world to come.[^s12][^s13][^s14]

## Geography

The Temple stood on a hill north of the old City of David, on the site David bought from Araunah (Ornan) the Jebusite; Chronicles identifies it as Mount Moriah (2 Chronicles 3:1).[^s5][^s18] Solomon's building faced east and had three rooms in a line: a porch, a main hall, and the inner sanctuary or Most Holy Place, a cube of twenty cubits that held the ark of the covenant beneath two olive-wood cherubim (1 Kings 6:2-3, 19-23).[^s3][^s15]

Herod's rebuilding, begun in his eighteenth year (about 20-19 BC), vastly enlarged the platform around the sanctuary.[^s16][^s17] An open outer court was open to everyone, including Gentiles; a barrier with warning notices barred non-Jews from the raised inner courts (the Court of Women and, beyond it, the men's court around the sanctuary); a curtain hung before the Holy of Holies.[^s17] Josephus says the priests built the sanctuary in eighteen months and the porticoes in eight years; the outer works went on for decades, so that in John 2:20 Jesus' hearers speak of forty-six years of building.[^s16][^s17][^s12] Nothing of Solomon's building has been recovered; its appearance is reconstructed from 1 Kings 6-7 and comparable ancient temples.[^s18] The great retaining walls of Herod's platform survive, the best known being the Western Wall, still a place of Jewish prayer and pilgrimage.[^s20]

## In the biblical story

**A house for the Name.** David, settled in a cedar palace, wanted to build a house for the ark; God answered that he had never asked for one, and instead promised David a dynasty whose heir would build it (2 Samuel 7:1-13).[^s2] Solomon began in his fourth year, "in the four hundred and eightieth year" after the Exodus, and finished in seven years (1 Kings 6:1, 37-38).[^s3] At the dedication the ark was brought into the inner sanctuary and a cloud filled the house, "for Yahweh's glory filled Yahweh's house" (1 Kings 8:6-11, WEB).[^s4] Yet Solomon's prayer guards against any idea that God could be boxed in: "heaven and the heaven of heavens can't contain you; how much less this house" (1 Kings 8:27, WEB). The Temple is a place to pray *toward*, trusting God to "hear in heaven" and forgive (1 Kings 8:28-30).[^s4]

**Destruction and rebuilding.** In Nebuchadnezzar's nineteenth year, his commander Nebuzaradan burned "Yahweh's house" and the Chaldeans broke up its bronze furnishings (2 Kings 25:8-13), in 586 BC.[^s6][^s20] Exiles returning from 538 BC set about rebuilding, and Haggai urged the people on, promising that "the latter glory of this house will be greater than the former" (Haggai 2:3-9, WEB).[^s8] The house was finished in the sixth year of Darius (Ezra 6:15), in 515 BC.[^s7][^s20] Antiochus IV's desecration of it in 167 BC sparked the Maccabean revolt, and Roman legions burned Herod's rebuilt Temple in AD 70.[^s20][^s17]

**Jesus and the Temple.** Jesus taught in the Temple courts[^s19] and drove out the traders, citing Scripture: "My house will be called a house of prayer for all the nations" (Mark 11:15-17, WEB).[^s9] He foretold that not one stone would be left on another (Mark 13:1-2), and at his death the curtain of the Temple "was torn in two from the top to the bottom" (Mark 15:38, WEB).[^s10][^s11] John adds that when Jesus said, "Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up," he meant his own body, as the disciples understood after the resurrection (John 2:18-22, WEB).[^s12] The Catholic Catechism sums up a view widely shared: Jesus honoured the Temple, yet presented himself as God's definitive dwelling among people.[^s19]

**A temple of people, and no temple at all.** Paul tells the Corinthian church, "ye are the temple of God, and ... the Spirit of God dwelleth in you" (1 Corinthians 3:16, KJV), using a plural "ye" for the whole congregation, which he has just called "God's building" (3:9).[^s13] Revelation's vision of the New Jerusalem ends the story with a surprise: John sees no temple there, "for the Lord God the Almighty and the Lamb are its temple" (Revelation 21:22, WEB).[^s14]

## Common misreadings

- **"God lived only in the Temple."** Solomon's own prayer denies it (1 Kings 8:27); it was where God put his name, not a container for him.[^s4]
- **"Jesus was against the Temple."** He taught there and called it his Father's house; his protest was against its misuse.[^s9][^s19]
- **"'You are God's temple' is mainly about the individual body."** In 1 Corinthians 3:16-17 the "ye" is plural and refers to the church as a community, not first to one person's body.[^s13]

## Connections

The Temple is part of **Jerusalem** and continues the story of the **tabernacle** (Exodus 25:8-9).[^s1][^s5] It **illustrates the presence of God**, since the cloud of glory filling the house at its dedication shows God dwelling among his people (1 Kings 8:10-11).[^s4] It also **illustrates the Davidic covenant**, because the promise that David's heir would build the house is the heart of 2 Samuel 7.[^s2] Read typologically, it **prefigures Jesus**, who identified himself with the Temple as God's dwelling (John 2:21).[^s19][^s12] Herod's rebuilding is **corroborated by Josephus's *Antiquities of the Jews***, our fullest ancient description of it.[^s16] It is **studied in biblical archaeology**, which reconstructs the First Temple from texts and comparable remains.[^s18]
