# Jerusalem

Jerusalem is the city the Bible keeps returning to, and the story of Scripture can be traced through it: a Canaanite stronghold, David's capital, the home of the Temple, a ruin in the exile, a rebuilt city, the place where Jesus died and rose, and finally a picture of God's renewed world in which he lives with his people.[^s15][^s16] Christians share its earliest story with the Jewish people, for whom the city has remained a focus of prayer, pilgrimage and hope down the centuries.[^s15]

## Why it matters

Jerusalem is where the Old Testament's promises gather: David's throne, the house of God, and the hope that the nations would one day come to learn God's ways (Isaiah 2:2-4).[^s9] In the Gospels it is where those promises come to a head in the cross and the empty tomb, and in Acts it is where the church's witness begins, "in Jerusalem, in all Judea and Samaria, and to the uttermost parts of the earth" (Acts 1:8, WEB).[^s11]

## Geography

The city sits on a spur of the Judean highlands, roughly 2,100 to 2,500 feet above sea level, with steep valleys on three sides: the Kidron Valley to the east, separating it from the Mount of Olives, the Valley of Hinnom to the west and south, and the Tyropoeon Valley running through the lower city.[^s15] Its only reliable natural water source was the Gihon Spring on the eastern slope.[^s15] That is why David's men could take the stronghold by way of "the watercourse" (2 Samuel 5:8, WEB), and why King Hezekiah, facing Assyria in the late eighth century BC, cut a tunnel to bring the spring's water inside the walls (2 Kings 20:20).[^s1][^s12][^s17] The oldest part of the city is the southeastern hill, the City of David; the Temple stood on the higher ground to its north.[^s15][^s17]

The city has many names. Genesis 14:18-20 tells of "Melchizedek king of Salem", priest of God Most High, who blessed Abram, and Psalm 76:2 sets Salem in parallel with Zion, which is why Jewish and Christian readers have long identified Salem with Jerusalem.[^s2][^s13][^s15] Before David it was called Jebus, after its Jebusite inhabitants; "Zion" first named the stronghold David captured and later the whole city, the Temple mount and God's people.[^s15][^s1]

## In the biblical story

**Before David.** Egyptian royal letters from long before David (the Amarna letters) include correspondence from Abdi-Heba, ruler of Jerusalem, asking Pharaoh for help, the city's earliest mention outside the Bible.[^s15] When Israel entered the land, the Jebusites remained in Jerusalem: "the children of Judah couldn't drive them out" (Joshua 15:63, WEB).[^s6]

**David and Solomon.** David reigned seven and a half years in Hebron, then took "the stronghold of Zion" and called it "David's city" (2 Samuel 5:4-9, WEB).[^s1] Solomon built the Temple "at Jerusalem on Mount Moriah" (2 Chronicles 3:1, WEB), linking the site to "the land of Moriah" where Abraham was sent with Isaac (Genesis 22:2).[^s5][^s14] Pilgrims sang Psalm 122 as they went up: "Pray for the peace of Jerusalem" (Psalm 122:6, WEB).[^s3]

**Destruction and return.** Nebuchadnezzar of Babylon took the city and burned the Temple in about 586 BC; exiles returned to rebuild the Temple, completed around 516 BC, and Nehemiah later rebuilt the walls.[^s15] Herod the Great restored the Temple from around 20 BC, and in AD 70 Roman armies under Titus destroyed the city again.[^s15]

**Jesus and the church.** From Luke 9:51 onward Jesus is on the road to the city: he "intently set his face to go to Jerusalem" (Luke 9:51, WEB).[^s10] As he approached, "he saw the city and wept over it", foreseeing its destruction (Luke 19:41-44, WEB).[^s4] There he was crucified and raised, and from there the apostles were sent out (Acts 1:8).[^s11]

**The Jerusalem above.** The prophets already pictured Jerusalem as the place from which "the word of Yahweh" would go out to the nations (Isaiah 2:3, WEB).[^s9] Paul contrasts "the Jerusalem that exists now" with "the Jerusalem that is above", which "is the mother of us all" (Galatians 4:25-26, WEB).[^s8] Revelation ends with "the holy city, New Jerusalem, coming down out of heaven from God", with no temple in it, "for the Lord God, the Almighty, and the Lamb, are its temple" (Revelation 21:2, 22, WEB).[^s7] The idea of a heavenly and an earthly Jerusalem runs through both Jewish and Christian thought.[^s16]

## Common misreadings

- **"Jerusalem was always an Israelite city."** The text says the Jebusites lived there until David, and the city was ruled by others for much of its later history.[^s6][^s1][^s15]
- **Zion as a different place.** In the Bible "Zion" and "Jerusalem" are usually two names for the same city, often in poetic parallel (Isaiah 2:3; Psalm 76:2).[^s9][^s13]
- **The heavenly city makes the earthly one unimportant.** Jesus wept over the real city, and the hope of a "Jerusalem above" grows out of God's faithfulness there.[^s4][^s8]

## Scholarly views

- **Salem.** Tradition identifies Melchizedek's Salem with Jerusalem; the Jewish Encyclopedia treats it as a likely short form of the name while noting other etymologies.[^s15][^s2]
- **The city of David and Solomon.** Scripture presents David's Jerusalem as a royal capital.[^s1] Eric Cline summarises a lively debate: some archaeologists see a substantial tenth-century city, others a small hill town; Eilat Mazar identified a "Large Stone Structure" as David's palace, while Israel Finkelstein and colleagues date parts of it later.[^s18] Bible Odyssey notes that later building limits excavation and that many structures once linked to David or Solomon proved later.[^s17]

## Connections

- Jerusalem **illustrates** the biblical theme of exile and return: burned by Babylon, its Temple and walls were rebuilt by returning exiles.[^s15]
- It is **corroborated by** the Amarna letters, whose Jerusalem correspondence is the city's earliest outside mention.[^s15]
- It is **studied in** biblical archaeology, where its early history remains much discussed.[^s17][^s18]
- It is **related** to the Temple, to David, to the Babylonian Exile and Ezra's return, and to the Council of Jerusalem.[^s1][^s5][^s15]
