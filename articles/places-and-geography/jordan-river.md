# Jordan River

The Jordan is the river of the Bible. It is the boundary Israel crossed to enter the promised land, the water Elijah and Elisha parted, the river in which Naaman the Syrian was healed, and the place where John baptized and Jesus was baptized.[^s1][^s3][^s4][^s5] Its Hebrew name, *Yarden*, is usually linked with the verb *yarad*, "to go down", and dictionaries gloss it as "the descender".[^s10][^s12][^s9]

## Why it matters

Again and again the Jordan marks a threshold: between wilderness and promised land, between one prophet and his successor, between Jesus' hidden years and his public ministry.[^s1][^s3][^s10] Picturing the real river, swift, winding and sunk deep below sea level, helps readers see why crossing it, washing in it, or seeing it turn back (Psalm 114:3) left such a mark on Israel's memory and on Christian worship.[^s8][^s10][^s11]

## Geography

The Jordan rises from springs at the foot of Mount Hermon, near Dan and Banias (Caesarea Philippi); the Jewish Encyclopedia counts three main streams and other dictionaries four.[^s9][^s10][^s11] Easton calls it "the chief river of Palestine".[^s12] It flows through the Huleh basin into the Sea of Galilee, then south to the Dead Sea, whose surface lies roughly 1,300 feet below sea level, the lowest body of water on earth.[^s10]

From the Sea of Galilee to the Dead Sea is only about 65 miles as the crow flies, but the river winds roughly 200 miles to cover it.[^s9][^s12] Its valley, the Ghor, holds a narrower flood plain, the Zor, overgrown with thickets that once sheltered lions and that flood at harvest time, just as Joshua 3:15 says.[^s10][^s11][^s1] There were no bridges before Roman times, only fords, which became places of ambush and battle.[^s10][^s9] Tributaries such as the Yarmuk and the Jabbok join from the east.[^s10][^s11]

## In the biblical story

**A well-watered plain.** The Jordan first appears when Lot looks over the plain of the Jordan, sees it well watered like the garden of the LORD, and chooses it for himself (Genesis 13:10-11).[^s7][^s12]

**Crossing into the land.** Israel camps at the Jordan, and the priests carry the ark of the covenant ahead of the people (Joshua 3:1-6). God tells Joshua, "Today I will begin to magnify you in the sight of all Israel" (Joshua 3:7, WEB).[^s1] When the priests' feet touch the flooded river, the water upstream stands in a heap at Adam, near Zarethan, and the people cross on dry ground opposite Jericho (Joshua 3:13-17).[^s1] Twelve men carry twelve stones from the riverbed to Gilgal, so that when children ask what they mean, their parents can tell them how God dried up the Jordan as he had dried up the Red Sea (Joshua 4:1-7, 19-24).[^s2] Psalm 114 sings the two crossings together: the sea fled and the Jordan turned back.[^s8]

**Prophets at the river.** Before he is taken up, Elijah strikes the Jordan with his cloak and he and Elisha cross on dry ground; Elisha asks for a double portion of his spirit, and after the chariot of fire separates them he takes up Elijah's cloak, strikes the water and crosses back (2 Kings 2:6-15).[^s3] Later Elisha tells the Syrian commander Naaman, "Go and wash in the Jordan seven times" (2 Kings 5:10, WEB). Naaman is offended, expecting a showier cure and asking whether the rivers of Damascus are not better than Israel's waters, but when he obeys his skin is restored, and he confesses that there is no God in all the earth but in Israel (2 Kings 5:11-15).[^s4]

**John and Jesus.** John the Baptist preaches in the wilderness of Judea, and people from Jerusalem, Judea and the whole region around the Jordan are baptized by him in the river, confessing their sins (Matthew 3:1-6).[^s5] Jesus comes from Galilee to be baptized; as he comes up from the water the heavens open, the Spirit descends like a dove, and a voice says, "This is my beloved Son, with whom I am well pleased" (Matthew 3:13-17, WEB).[^s5] John's Gospel places John's baptizing at Bethany beyond the Jordan, where he points to Jesus as the Lamb of God (John 1:28-34).[^s6] As one dictionary puts it, Jesus' public ministry began at the Jordan.[^s10]

## Common misreadings

- **"Crossing Jordan" means dying.** Hymns and spirituals often use the river as an image of passing through death into heaven.[^s10] It can be a fruitful image, but in Joshua the crossing is about entering the land God promised, and in the Gospels the Jordan is a place of repentance and new beginning, not of death.[^s1][^s5]
- **The baptism site is certain.** Several traditional sites claim it, and the Gospels give only general locations (see below).[^s10][^s13]

## Scholarly views

- **Where was Jesus baptized?** Most early manuscripts of John 1:28 read "Bethany", but Origen, who says he visited the area, argued the text should read "Bethabara", a place on the riverbank, and explained the name as "House of preparation".[^s14] The Encyclopedia of the Bible notes that Matthew suggests the southern stretch near Jericho, while Mark's account could suggest a place nearer Galilee.[^s10] UNESCO's World Heritage listing, on the east bank north of the Dead Sea, describes the site as "believed to be the location where Jesus of Nazareth was baptized", a Christian pilgrimage place also associated with Elijah's ascension.[^s13]
- **How did the waters stop?** Christians and Jews have read Joshua 3 as a miracle of God; rabbinic tradition even pictured the water piled miles high.[^s9][^s1] Some scholars note that collapsing banks near Adam (modern Damiya) have blocked the river in recorded history, as recently as 1927, and suggest God may have worked through such an event; the text itself stresses the timing, at the moment the priests stepped in.[^s10][^s11][^s1]

## Connections

- The Jordan **illustrates** baptism: John's baptism of repentance and Jesus' own baptism took place in its water.[^s5]
- It **illustrates** redemption: Joshua presents the crossing as a second Red Sea, God bringing his people out of slavery and into their inheritance.[^s2]
- It is **studied in** biblical archaeology, notably at the east-bank baptism site with its churches, pools and pilgrim station.[^s13]
- It sits within the land of Israel and is related to Galilee, Joshua, Elijah, Elisha, John the Baptist and the baptism of Jesus.[^s1][^s3][^s5]
