# Sadducees

The Sadducees were a small but powerful Jewish party in the last two centuries before the Temple's destruction in AD 70. They were drawn mostly from Jerusalem's wealthy, priestly elite, they held to the written law of Moses rather than the Pharisees' inherited traditions, and they did not believe in a resurrection of the dead.[^s15][^s16] Christians meet them chiefly in two places: in the Gospels, where they try to trap Jesus with a riddle about the resurrection, and in Acts, where the high priest and "the sect of the Sadducees" arrest the apostles (Acts 5:17, WEB).[^s1][^s7]

## Why it matters

The Sadducees show that first-century Judaism held several schools in lively debate, and that Jesus spoke into real questions of his day.[^s13][^s15] Their questions also give us one of Jesus's few direct arguments for the resurrection, drawn from the Books of Moses themselves.[^s1] And because they held the Temple's highest offices, they help explain why the earliest preaching of the risen Jesus in Jerusalem met such quick and official resistance.[^s6][^s15]

## Who they were

The name most likely means "Zadokites," after Zadok, whom Solomon appointed priest in place of Abiathar (1 Kings 2:35); his descendants led the Temple priesthood for centuries.[^s11][^s16] Other proposals link it to the Hebrew for "righteous" or a Greek term for officials; none is certain.[^s15] The party appears in Josephus's account of the Hasmonean era, and it became closely tied to the high priesthood when the ruler John Hyrcanus turned from the Pharisees to the Sadducees.[^s15]

Josephus describes three Jewish "schools": Pharisees, Sadducees and Essenes.[^s13] In his telling the Sadducees left good and evil entirely to human choice and rejected the idea that the soul survives death to face reward or punishment; they held "that souls die with the bodies" (Antiquities 18.16).[^s12][^s13] They kept only what the written law commanded and counted it a virtue to dispute with their teachers.[^s13] Josephus adds that their views were held by few, though those few were men of the highest rank, and elsewhere that they had the confidence of the wealthy rather than the ordinary people; he also found them rough in their dealings even with one another.[^s13][^s15][^s12] Acts adds that they denied the resurrection, angels and spirits (Acts 23:8).[^s8]

With their power rooted in the Temple and its offices, the Sadducees disappear from history after the Romans destroyed Jerusalem and its sanctuary.[^s15][^s16]

## In the biblical story

**Questioning Jesus about the resurrection.** All three Synoptic Gospels record the Sadducees' question (Mark 12:18-27; Matthew 22:23-34; Luke 20:27-40). They build a case on the law of levirate marriage, in which a man's brother marries his widow to raise up an heir in his name (Deuteronomy 25:5-6): if a woman outlives seven brothers in turn, whose wife will she be at the resurrection?[^s1][^s5] Jesus replies that they are mistaken, "not knowing the Scriptures nor the power of God" (Mark 12:24, WEB). The risen do not marry, and God's words to Moses at the bush, "I am the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob" (Matthew 22:32, WEB, citing Exodus 3:6), show that "he is not the God of the dead, but of the living" (Mark 12:27, WEB).[^s1][^s2][^s4] Some scribes answered, "Teacher, you speak well" (Luke 20:39, WEB), and Matthew says Jesus had "silenced the Sadducees" (Matthew 22:34, WEB).[^s3][^s2]

**Other Gospel scenes.** John the Baptist warns Pharisees and Sadducees who come to his baptism (Matthew 3:7), and the two groups together ask Jesus for a sign from heaven; he then warns his disciples against their "yeast," which Matthew explains as their teaching (Matthew 16:1-12).[^s9][^s10]

**Opposing the apostles.** In Acts the priests, the captain of the Temple and the Sadducees arrest Peter and John, upset that they "proclaimed in Jesus the resurrection from the dead" (Acts 4:2, WEB).[^s6] Later the high priest and his party, "the sect of the Sadducees," jail the apostles (Acts 5:17-18).[^s7] Before the council Paul declares that he is on trial for the hope of the resurrection, and the Pharisees and Sadducees fall into such a dispute that a Roman officer has to rescue him (Acts 23:6-10).[^s8] Josephus adds that the Sadducee high priest Ananus the younger had James, "the brother of Jesus, who was called Christ," stoned (Antiquities 20.200).[^s14]

## Scholarly views

Historians agree on the outline above, but much remains uncertain because nearly everything we know comes from the Sadducees' critics: Josephus, the New Testament and later rabbinic writings, which sometimes use "Sadducee" loosely for heretics in general.[^s16] Some early church writers thought the Sadducees accepted only the five Books of Moses; many scholars think it more likely they accepted the wider Scriptures but gave the Law of Moses first place and rejected unwritten tradition.[^s15] Scholars also debate whether the group is best described by its priestly lineage or by its aristocratic social standing, since some members were wealthy laymen; and whether its denial of angels meant rejecting angels entirely or only the elaborate angel lore of other groups.[^s15][^s16]

## Common misreadings

It is easy to lump "the Pharisees and Sadducees" together as a single bloc. The Gospels sometimes pair them, but they disagreed sharply about the resurrection, tradition and authority, as Acts 23 shows.[^s8][^s10] Nor were the Sadducees simple sceptics; they were devoted to the Temple and to the Law of Moses as they understood it.[^s16] Their story warns believers that knowing the Bible's words is not the same as trusting the living God who speaks in them.[^s1]

## Connections

The Sadducees **contrast with** the Pharisees, above all over the resurrection, angels and spirits (Acts 23:6-10).[^s8] Josephus also sets them alongside and against the Essenes as one of Judaism's three schools.[^s13] Their beliefs and standing are **attested outside the Bible** in Josephus's *Antiquities of the Jews*.[^s13] They are **explained by** Second Temple Judaism and by their bond with the Temple, whose fall ended the party.[^s15][^s16] They are tied to the resurrection of the dead, the Pentateuch they prized, and the stories of Mark and Acts.[^s1][^s6]
