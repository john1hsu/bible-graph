# Jesus

Jesus of Nazareth is the person the whole New Testament is about, and the one Christians believe the Old Testament was moving towards. The Gospels tell of a Jewish man from Galilee who announced that God's kingdom had come near, healed and taught, gathered disciples, was crucified in Jerusalem under the Roman governor Pontius Pilate and, his followers testified, rose from the dead.[^s3][^s6][^s11] His name is the Greek form of Joshua and carries the sense "saviour"; Matthew explains it with the angel's words, "he who shall save his people from their sins" (Matthew 1:21, WEB).[^s13][^s1] "Christ" is not a surname but a title: Greek *christos*, "anointed one", translating the Hebrew word we know as Messiah.[^s11][^s13]

## Why it matters

Every other article in this graph connects to Jesus somehow. Christians read the Law, the Prophets and the Psalms as Scripture that speaks of him, because the risen Jesus himself opened them that way: "Beginning from Moses and from all the prophets, he explained to them in all the Scriptures the things concerning himself" (Luke 24:27, WEB).[^s7] Knowing who he is, and where he stands in the story, is the key to reading the whole Bible as one book.[^s7][^s8]

## Life and story

**Birth and childhood.** Luke tells how Joseph, "of the house and family of David", went from Nazareth to Bethlehem for a Roman census, and how Mary laid her newborn son in a feeding trough (Luke 2:1-7).[^s2] Historians place the birth around 6–4 BC, before the death of Herod the Great.[^s11] Jesus grew up in Nazareth in Galilee, where neighbours later knew him as "the carpenter, the son of Mary" (Mark 6:3, WEB).[^s2][^s10] The only glimpse of his boyhood is Luke's account of a Passover pilgrimage at twelve, when his parents found him in the temple listening to the teachers and asking them questions (Luke 2:41-52).[^s2][^s11] He grew up within the faith and festivals of Israel.

**Baptism and ministry.** Jesus came from Nazareth to be baptized by John in the Jordan; the Spirit descended on him and a voice said, "You are my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased" (Mark 1:9-11, WEB).[^s3] After forty days in the wilderness he began preaching in Galilee: "The time is fulfilled, and God's Kingdom is at hand! Repent, and believe in the Good News" (Mark 1:15, WEB).[^s3] In the Nazareth synagogue he read from the scroll of Isaiah about the anointed one who brings good news to the poor and announced, "Today, this Scripture has been fulfilled in your hearing" (Luke 4:21, WEB).[^s4] The span of this work is traced in [The Public Ministry of Jesus](bg:public-ministry-of-jesus).

**Confession and cross.** At Caesarea Philippi Jesus asked, "But who do you say that I am?" and Peter answered, "You are the Christ, the Son of the living God" (Matthew 16:15-16, WEB).[^s5] From that time Jesus began to show his disciples that he must go to Jerusalem, suffer, be killed and be raised on the third day (Matthew 16:21).[^s5] He was crucified under Pilate around AD 30.[^s11][^s14]

**Resurrection.** Paul hands on what he himself received: that Christ died for our sins, was buried, was raised on the third day "according to the Scriptures", and appeared to Cephas, to the Twelve, to more than five hundred at once, to James, to all the apostles and finally to Paul (1 Corinthians 15:3-8).[^s6]

## Legacy in Scripture

The New Testament writers reach for the highest language they have. John opens with the Word who was with God and was God, through whom all things were made, and who "became flesh and lived among us" (John 1:1-3, 14, WEB).[^s8] Paul's hymn in Philippians describes Christ, existing in the form of God, emptying himself, taking the form of a servant and becoming obedient to death on a cross, so that every tongue should confess "that Jesus Christ is Lord" (Philippians 2:6-11, WEB).[^s9] Luke shows the risen Jesus teaching that everything written about him in the law of Moses, the prophets and the psalms had to be fulfilled, and that repentance and forgiveness should be preached in his name to all nations (Luke 24:44-47).[^s7]

The church summed up this witness in its creeds. The Nicene Creed confesses one Lord, Jesus Christ, the only Son of God, who "became truly human", was crucified under Pontius Pilate, rose on the third day and will come again in glory.[^s16] The Council of Chalcedon (451) described him as one person, "truly God and truly man", in two natures.[^s17] Most Christians, across Catholic, Orthodox and Protestant churches, regard Jesus as God incarnate.[^s11][^s16]

## Jesus in history

Christian faith rests on a real person in real history. Luke dates events by emperors and governors (Luke 2:1-2).[^s2] Non-Christian writers mention him too: the Roman historian Tacitus says that Christus "suffered the extreme penalty" under Tiberius at the hands of Pontius Pilate (*Annals* 15.44), and the Jewish historian Josephus refers to James as "the brother of Jesus, who was called Christ" (*Antiquities* 20.200).[^s14][^s15] Historians also test the Gospel traditions with criteria such as multiple attestation and dissimilarity. Yale's Dale Martin, applying them, calls Jesus's baptism by John and the charge "King of the Jews" "certain historical events", and describes Jesus as a Jewish peasant who spoke mainly Aramaic.[^s12] Beyond such historical judgments, faith confesses more, as the creeds do.[^s16]

## Common misreadings

- **"Christ" as a surname.** It is a title, "the Anointed One"; to say "Jesus Christ" is to say "Jesus the Messiah", which is exactly what Peter confessed.[^s11][^s5]
- **A Jesus cut off from Israel.** Jesus was born into David's family line, kept Passover, read Isaiah in the synagogue, and is best understood within the Judaism of his day.[^s2][^s4][^s12]
- **Only a wise teacher.** The Gospels do record great teaching, but they centre on his identity, death and resurrection; Jesus himself asks "who do you say that I am?"[^s5][^s6]

## Connections

Jesus **illustrates** the [kingdom of God](bg:kingdom-of-god), which he announced as "at hand" (Mark 1:15).[^s3] Luke presents him as **fulfilling** the [Book of Isaiah](bg:book-of-isaiah) when he reads Isaiah's words in Nazareth and says they are fulfilled today.[^s4] He is **explained by** [Second Temple Judaism](bg:second-temple-judaism), the Jewish world in which he lived and taught.[^s12] He is **corroborated by** Tacitus's [*Annals*](bg:annals-of-tacitus) and Josephus's [*Antiquities of the Jews*](bg:antiquities-of-the-jews).[^s14][^s15] The Gospels, his public ministry, his crucifixion and resurrection, his disciples [Peter](bg:peter) and [Paul](bg:paul), and his mother [Mary](bg:mary-mother-of-jesus) and forerunner [John the Baptist](bg:john-the-baptist) are all part of his story.[^s3][^s5][^s6][^s10]
