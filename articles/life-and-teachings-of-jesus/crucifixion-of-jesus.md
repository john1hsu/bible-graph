# Crucifixion of Jesus

The crucifixion of Jesus is his execution on a Roman cross at a place called Golgotha, "The Place of a Skull", near Jerusalem, after he was handed over to the governor Pontius Pilate.[^s1][^s2] All four Gospels tell it in detail, and Paul hands on the early creed "that Christ died for our sins according to the Scriptures" (1 Corinthians 15:3, WEB).[^s4] Churches East and West still confess in the Nicene Creed that "for our sake he was crucified under Pontius Pilate".[^s16]

## Why it matters

The cross sits at the centre of the Christian story. Paul wrote that he preached "Christ crucified", a message that looked like a stumbling block to some and foolishness to others, yet is "the power of God and the wisdom of God" to those who are called (1 Corinthians 1:23-24, WEB).[^s7] Christ "humbled himself, becoming obedient to death, yes, the death of the cross" (Philippians 2:8, WEB).[^s6]

## Background: crucifixion in the Roman world

Rome used crucifixion for criminals and agitators; it was the typical punishment of slaves, thought the most shameful of deaths, and Roman citizens were exempt.[^s13][^s14] The condemned was usually flogged first and made to carry the crossbeam; a placard (*titulus*) named the charge; hands and feet were tied or nailed; death often came through heart failure or asphyxiation.[^s13][^s14] Constantine abolished the penalty in the fourth century out of reverence for Christ's Passion.[^s13]

Archaeology adds one rare witness: a heel bone still pierced by an iron nail, found in 1968 at Giv'at ha-Mivtar in Jerusalem in the ossuary of "Yehohanan son of Hagkol", from before AD 70 — proof that a crucified man could be laid in a Jewish tomb.[^s15]

Deuteronomy 21:23 says a hanged man's body must not remain on the tree overnight, and calls him accursed.[^s8] John notes that the bodies were not to remain on the cross because it was the Preparation Day,[^s2] and Paul echoes the verse when he writes that Christ "redeemed us from the curse of the law, having become a curse for us" (Galatians 3:13, WEB).[^s5]

## What happened

**Before Pilate.** In the morning the chief priests, elders, scribes and council bound Jesus and delivered him to Pilate, who asked whether he was "the King of the Jews". Pilate released the insurrectionist Barabbas, had Jesus flogged, and handed him over to be crucified (Mark 15:1-15).[^s1] Luke adds a hearing before Herod Antipas, who mocked Jesus and sent him back (Luke 23:7-11).[^s3]

**The way to Golgotha.** Soldiers dressed Jesus in purple, pressed on a crown of thorns and mocked him (Mark 15:16-20). Simon of Cyrene was compelled to carry the cross; John says Jesus went out bearing it himself (Mark 15:21; John 19:17).[^s1][^s2]

**On the cross.** Mark dates it to the third hour. The charge read "THE KING OF THE JEWS", and two robbers were crucified with him (Mark 15:25-27).[^s1] John says Pilate had the title written in Hebrew, Latin and Greek, and that the soldiers cast lots for his clothing "that the Scripture might be fulfilled", quoting Psalm 22:18 (John 19:19-24).[^s2][^s9] Darkness covered the land from the sixth to the ninth hour, and Jesus cried, "My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?" — the opening line of Psalm 22 (Mark 15:33-34).[^s1][^s9]

**Words from the cross.** Luke records "Father, forgive them, for they don't know what they are doing", the promise "today you will be with me in Paradise", and "Father, into your hands I commit my spirit!" (Luke 23:34, 43, 46, WEB).[^s3] John records Jesus entrusting his mother to the beloved disciple and his final "It is finished!" (John 19:26-27, 30).[^s2]

**Death and burial.** When Jesus died, the temple veil was torn in two, and the centurion confessed, "Truly this man was the Son of God!"; Luke has him call Jesus righteous (Mark 15:37-39; Luke 23:45-47).[^s1][^s3] Women, among them Mary Magdalene, watched from a distance. Joseph of Arimathea, a respected council member, asked for the body and laid it in a rock-cut tomb; John adds Nicodemus and a new tomb in a garden (Mark 15:40-46; John 19:38-42).[^s1][^s2]

## Significance

The New Testament never treats the cross as a tragic accident. Paul's early summary links Jesus's death, burial and resurrection on the third day, all "according to the Scriptures" (1 Corinthians 15:3-4).[^s4] Psalm 22, whose first line Jesus prays, moves from abandonment to praise: God "heard" the sufferer, and "the kingdom is Yahweh's".[^s9] The torn veil and the centurion's confession invite readers to see God's purposes revealed in this death.[^s1]

How the cross reconciles us to God is described with many biblical images — ransom, sacrifice, the Passover lamb, the curse borne — and traditions emphasise these differently; that is the subject of the atonement.[^s5][^s18] The Catholic Catechism, for example, teaches that Christ's death was part of God's plan and was offered freely for every human being.[^s18]

## Reading it well

- **Don't blame "the Jews".** Jesus, his disciples, the women at the cross and Joseph of Arimathea were all Jews; the penalty was Roman, and Pilate gave the order.[^s1][^s13] The Catholic Church's *Nostra Aetate* says what happened in the Passion "cannot be charged against all the Jews, without distinction, then alive", nor against Jews today,[^s17] and the Catechism teaches that sinners were "the authors and the ministers" of Christ's sufferings.[^s18]
- **Hear the whole psalm.** Jesus's cry is not a loss of faith but the opening of a prayer that ends in trust.[^s9]
- **Notice the restraint.** The Gospels say simply that "they crucified him"; their focus is on who Jesus is, not on graphic detail.[^s1]

## Historical attestation and date

Two writers outside the New Testament mention the crucifixion. The Roman historian Tacitus writes that Christus suffered execution under Tiberius at the hands of the procurator Pontius Pilate.[^s10] The Jewish historian Josephus, in *Antiquities* 18.63-64 as it has come down to us, says that Pilate condemned Jesus to the cross at the suggestion of leading men, and that his followers did not abandon him.[^s11] Pilate governed Judea from AD 26 to 36. Because the Gospels place the death on a Friday at Passover, astronomical calculation narrows the likely years to AD 30 or 33; Humphreys and Waddington argue for Friday 3 April AD 33 and read John's timing — the day before Passover — as the historical one, while acknowledging AD 30 remains possible.[^s12]

## Connections

- The crucifixion **features** [Jesus](bg:jesus) and [Pontius Pilate](bg:pontius-pilate), who handed him over to be crucified (Mark 15:15).[^s1]
- It **occurred at** Golgotha, which John places near the city of [Jerusalem](bg:jerusalem).[^s2]
- It **precedes** the [resurrection of Jesus](bg:resurrection-of-jesus): "he was buried ... raised on the third day" (1 Corinthians 15:4).[^s4]
- It **fulfils** [Psalm 22](bg:psalm-22), which John quotes at the dividing of the garments.[^s2]
- It **illustrates** the [atonement](bg:atonement): Christ "died for our sins".[^s4]
- It is **corroborated by** Tacitus's [*Annals*](bg:annals-of-tacitus)[^s10] and Josephus's [*Antiquities of the Jews*](bg:antiquities-of-the-jews).[^s11]
- It is **explained by** [Passover](bg:passover), the feast during which it took place.[^s12]
